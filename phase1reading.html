Phase 1

Building Reading (Receptive Literacy)

Focus on helping her understand that written text represents meaningful ideas, starting entirely within her realm of interest.

Because Gestalt Language Processors (GLPs) process written print as whole visual chunks rather than individual letters, this 5-level framework evaluates how effectively she recognizes, connects, and extracts meaning from visual words — and gives you activities to help her move from one level to the next.

Phase 1 Evaluation Framework: 5 Skill Levels

A quick map of the whole journey, from not noticing print at all to reading full Gestalt sentence strips.

1

Print Unaware

Treats printed text as background noise or decoration; does not look at words when pointed out or show interest in book print / labels.

2

Emergent Print Connection

Notices printed labels or familiar logos (e.g., Disney, YouTube, snack brand logos); occasionally tracks a finger pointing to text when accompanied by an enthusiastic voice.

3

High-Motivation Target Recognizer

Consistently recognizes 3–5 high-value printed words (e.g., Juice, Trampoline, Bluey) when paired with pictures or real items. Demonstrates basic left-to-right visual tracking.

4

Generalizing Receptive Reader

Receptively identifies 8–15+ target words across different contexts (cards, screens, book pages) without requiring the physical object present; matches printed word cards to pictures/objects accurately.

5

Receptive Master (Phase 1 Expert)

Understands multi-word Gestalt visual strips (e.g., [ I want ] + [ Water ]); receptively selects or points to correct words in a sequence to fulfill a desire; reads whole phrases without needing picture support.

Bridging Level 1 → Level 2

Core Focus: First Contact With Print

Goal

Get her eyes to land on a printed shape for even 1 to 2 seconds and realize "that shape gets me what I want."

Rule

No multi-word cards, no fast video subtitles, and no pressure to speak or match.

1

The "Cover-Up" Barrier (Physical Interaction)

Setup

Take 3–4 post-it notes or index cards. On each card, write ONE single, giant, bold word in thick black marker for an item she loves (e.g., JUICE, IPAD, SWING).

How to play

  1. Cover her iPad screen or snack bowl directly with the IPAD or JUICE post-it note.
  2. She will naturally reach to peel or push the post-it off to get her item.
  3. Right as her hand touches the post-it, tap the text, say the word in a clear, happy, single tone ("Juice!"), and let her remove it.

Why this works

She physically has to look at and touch the printed word to access what she wants. Print becomes a physical key, not just something passive on a wall.

2

The High-Contrast "Peek-a-Boo" Reveal

Setup

Write a favorite target word (BLUEY, BUBBLES) in giant black letters on a plain white card. Hide the actual item behind a small blanket or box.

How to play

  1. Hold the white text card directly over the hidden item.
  2. Shake the card gently to catch her visual attention.
  3. As soon as her eyes glance at the card, say the word in a fun, exaggerated voice ("BUBBLES!"), immediately pull away the blanket, and blow the bubbles.

Why this works

The movement of the card and the immediate physical "payoff" (bubbles appearing) teaches her brain that the white card with black marks predicts fun.

3

Tactile & Sensory Word Tracing

Setup

Write her top favorite word (PARK or her name) on a tray using a sensory medium she enjoys — like finger-paint, whipped cream, kinetic sand, or a squishy gel bag.

How to play

  1. Do not ask her to write or trace.
  2. Put her favorite toy right next to the tray.
  3. Take your own finger and trace through the letters smoothly, making a fun sound effect (like a car revving or a slide sound: "Wooooosh... PARK!").
  4. Let her smash, touch, or run her fingers through the textured word.

Why this works

It removes the abstract "paper" element and lets her experience letter shapes through tactile touch and play.

Bridging Level 2 → Level 3

Core Focus: Choosing Between Words

Goal

Transition from reacting to single isolated text cards to choosing the correct printed word when given options.

Rule

Focus strictly on 3 to 5 of her highest-motivation items. Never use random or uninspiring words (like dog, cat, or hat).

1

The "Visual Choice" Swap (2-Card Choice)

Setup

Create two distinct word cards with bold black text: one for a high-value item (JUICE) and one for a neutral/unpreferred item (SHOE).

How to play

  1. Hold both items out of reach, but place the two word cards directly in front of her.
  2. Say in a warm, inviting tone: "What do you want?" (Do not read the cards for her yet).
  3. Point to the JUICE card, sweep your finger left-to-right under it, and read it using high melodic pitch: "Juuuice!" Then point to SHOE in a flat tone: "Shoe."
  4. Prompt her to touch or hand you one card. If she picks JUICE, hand her the juice immediately. If she picks SHOE, hand her the shoe calmly.

Why this works

She quickly connects the specific visual shape of the word JUICE with her favorite outcome.

2

"Word-to-Object" Matching Puzzles

Setup

Take 3 clear plastic bins or placemats. Tape her top 3 target word cards (IPAD, BUBBLES, SNACK) onto the front of each bin/mat in giant, clear lettering.

How to play

  1. Hand her the physical object (e.g., the jar of bubbles).
  2. Tap the BUBBLES word on the bin, sweep your finger under it, and say "Bubbles!"
  3. Guide her hand to place the bubbles into the bin labeled BUBBLES.
  4. Repeat daily during play or snack time until she drops the items into the correctly labeled bins independently.

Why this works

It anchors the 3D physical object directly to its 2D written representation through a concrete sorting motion.

3

The Left-to-Right "Sing-Song Sweep"

Setup

Use her top 3 target word cards (SWING, MUSIC, WATER).

How to play

  1. Before giving her the requested item, hold the card at her eye level.
  2. Use a bright, contrasting pointer (like a light-up wand, a favorite toy figure, or a brightly colored finger puppet).
  3. Place the pointer at the first letter, then sweep smoothly across to the last letter while singing/stretching out the word’s gestalt rhythm (e.g., "Swiiiing!" or "Mu-sic!").
  4. Hand her the pointer and let her "swipe" across the card herself before giving her the item.

Why this works

The sensory pointer and melodic sweeping train her eyes to track across the whole visual gestalt from left to right.

Bridging Level 3 → Level 4

Core Focus: Reading Pure Text

Goal

Receptively identify 10–15+ target words from pure text alone (no picture prompts or physical items present during the choice) and recognize those words across different fonts/settings.

Rule

Expand beyond immediate snacks/toys to include action words, locations, and high-frequency routines (GO, OPEN, PARK, CAR, ALL DONE).

1

The "Pure Text" Treasure Hunt (Fading Pictures)

Setup

Take 3 to 4 of her known target word cards (MUSIC, SNACK, BUBBLES). Keep the actual items hidden in cupboards or drawers out of view.

How to play

  1. Lay out 3 text-only cards on the table.
  2. Tell her in an excited tone: "Let’s find something!"
  3. Point to or hand her one specific card (e.g., BUBBLES) without pointing toward where the item is.
  4. Have her carry the text card with her across the room to the cupboard where the bubbles are kept to "trade" the card for the item.

Why this works

She has to hold the pure visual representation of the word in her working memory without seeing the physical item first.

2

The "Multi-Choice" Table Matrix (3–4 Distractors)

Setup

Create 4 text-only cards (e.g., WATER, JUICE, MILK, APPLE).

How to play

  1. Lay all 4 cards flat on the table in a grid.
  2. Ask her: "Where says JUICE?" or play a sound snippet related to one item.
  3. Do not point, give eye-gaze cues, or show a picture.
  4. Wait up to 5–8 seconds for her to visually scan the 4 options and tap/hand you the correct word card.
  5. Celebrate with high enthusiasm when she isolates the correct target amidst 3 distractors!
3

Font & Environment Generalization ("Same Word, Different Look")

Setup

Choose 3 of her master sight words. Prepare them in 3 different formats: a print card (sans-serif font like Arial), a digital screen (typed on a tablet notes app), and a whiteboard/chalkboard (handwritten in block letters).

How to play

  1. Show her the word on the whiteboard (PARK).
  2. Ask her to match it to the same word typed on the tablet screen.
  3. Practice identifying her target words when out in public (e.g., pointing to the word EXIT or PUSH on doors, or PARK on a park sign).

Why this works

It breaks the rigidity of memorizing a single flashcard and teaches true abstract text processing.

Bridging Level 4 → Level 5 · Phase 1 Mastery

Core Focus: Reading Whole Gestalt Phrases

Goal

Receptively comprehend multi-word Gestalt visual strips, track two-to-three word sequences left-to-right, and select target words to complete functional sentence blocks.

Rule

Always keep carrier phrases consistent (e.g., use I WANT, I SEE, LET'S GO) so her brain memorizes the carrier block and focuses her attention on the changing target word.

1

The "Velcro Carrier Strip" Builder

Setup

Create a rigid base board (a strip of laminate with velcro dots) divided into two slots: Slot 1 (fixed carrier gestalt) holds a printed card that says I WANT or LET'S GO; Slot 2 (target word choice) is a blank velcro spot.

How to play

  1. Place the fixed card I WANT in Slot 1.
  2. Lay out 3 text-only target cards on the table (WATER, SNACK, MUSIC).
  3. Ask: "What do we want?"
  4. She selects the target word (e.g., WATER) and sticks it into Slot 2.
  5. Sweep your finger across both blocks continuously left-to-right: "I WANT WATER!" and fulfill the request immediately.

Why this works

It teaches her how independent word blocks snap together visually to convey a complete multi-word thought.

2

Receptive Sentence Discrimination ("Match the Phrase")

Setup

Create two distinct carrier sentence strips: Strip A — [ LET'S GO ] [ OUTSIDE ]; Strip B — [ I WANT ] [ MUSIC ].

How to play

  1. Place both constructed strips side-by-side on the table.
  2. Point to Strip A and read it aloud with high intonation ("LET’S GO OUTSIDE!"). Point to Strip B and read it ("I WANT MUSIC!").
  3. Stand by the door or hold the iPad, and ask: "Which one says ‘Let’s go outside’?"
  4. She scans both strips, evaluates the entire sequence of text blocks, and hands you or points to Strip A.

Why this works

She is no longer just looking at one isolated word; her brain is processing multi-word sentence structure receptively.

3

"Fill-in-the-Blank" Gestalt Reading Books

Setup

Create a simple 4-page mini photo book using pictures of her favorite things/activities. On the bottom of each page, print a sentence frame with a missing blank, e.g. Page 1: "Look at the [ ____ ]!" (picture of Bluey); Page 2: "Look at the [ ____ ]!" (picture of a trampoline).

How to play

  1. Open Page 1. Hand her a small tray with 3 printed choice cards (BLUEY, JUICE, PARK).
  2. Read the page while sweeping your finger under the print: "Look at the..." (pause at the blank).
  3. She selects the BLUEY card and drops or tapes it directly into the blank frame.
  4. Read the whole completed sentence together aloud to finish the page before turning to the next one!
