Phase 2

Building Writing (Expressive Literacy)

Writing for a non-verbal child does not need to start with handwriting or holding a pencil, which can present motor-planning (dyspraxia) hurdles. Start with low-tech or digital construction tools instead.

This 5-level framework moves from sensory scribbling with no intentional structure, all the way to spontaneous, independent written thoughts — with activities and checklists for every transition in between.

Phase 2 Evaluation Framework: 5 Skill Levels

A quick map of the whole journey, from random sensory marks to independently written multi-word thoughts.

1

Pre-Motor / Random Scribbler

Makes marks on paper or taps on a keyboard, but without intentional letter/word structure; treats writing tools purely as sensory objects (banging crayons, scribbling off the page).

2

Target Constructive Communicator

Uses physical drag-and-drop or velcro word blocks to independently select and place 1 single target word to express a direct desire or thought.

3

Structured Frame Builder

Assembles 2-to-3 word Gestalt phrases independently using physical word tiles or an AAC/digital grid (e.g., placing [ I WANT ] + [ JUICE ] together to make a request).

4

Emergent Letter & Word Producer

Traces, copies, or types out familiar target words (like her name or top interest words) using adaptive grip tools, keyboard typing, or sensory tracing mediums.

5

Independent Expressive Writer (Phase 2 Master)

Spontaneously constructs original multi-word thoughts, answers questions, or writes stories using alternative writing setups (keyboard, word banks, or handwriting) without visual templates or prompts.

Find Her Starting Point

Check off the behaviors she displays consistently. The highest level where all or most boxes are checked is her baseline for Phase 2.

Level 1 → Level 2

From Sensory Scribbling to Single-Word Placement

Goal

Bypassing motor frustration. Teaching her that physically picking up or sliding a 2D word tile expresses a specific thought.

Rule

Use thick, solid wooden tiles, velcro cards, or high-contrast tablet apps. Zero emphasis on pencil grip.

1

The "Peel & Place" Requesting Board

Setup

A laminated board with a velcro strip on the bottom. Keep 2 target word tiles (JUICE, PARK) on top.

How to play

  1. When she wants an item, guide her to peel the specific tile (JUICE) from the top and stick it onto the bottom strip before handing you the board.
  2. Immediately fulfill the request.
2

The "Sliding Tile" Drag-and-Drop

Setup

On a tablet screen (or magnetic whiteboard), place 2 large word blocks at the top (SWING, MUSIC) and a target box at the bottom.

How to play

  1. Have her use her index finger to physically slide the correct word block straight down into the target box to activate the music or activity.
3

Sensory Name & Word Stamping

Setup

Large letter stamps or rubber word blocks with a washable ink pad / play-dough.

How to play

  1. Let her press full word stamps directly into play-dough or paper.
  2. This bridges physical touch with creating clear printed text output effortlessly.

Level 2 → Level 3

From Single Words to Sentence Strip Construction

Goal

Constructing multi-word Gestalt phrases by joining blocks together left-to-right.

Rule

Carrier blocks ([ I WANT ], [ I SEE ], [ LET'S GO ]) stay intact as single unified pieces.

1

The "Snap-Together" Sentence Train

Setup

Use interlocking blocks (like Duplo/Lego) with printed word labels taped to the side. Green block = [ I WANT ], Blue block = [ WATER ].

How to play

  1. Guide her to snap the Green carrier block and Blue target block together side-by-side from left to right.
  2. Once snapped together, read the full phrase aloud and hand her the water.
2

Predictable Chart Writing (Fill-in-the-Blank)

Setup

A large poster chart with repetitive fixed lines, e.g. "I SEE [ _____ ]." repeated down the page.

How to play

  1. During a walk or video, pause and give her 3 target word tiles (BLUEY, CAR, DOG).
  2. She places her chosen tile into the blank box to finish the line, and you read the completed page together.
3

Digital Grid Construction (Low-Tech AAC / Text-to-Speech)

Setup

Use a tablet app set to a high-contrast grid where buttons contain full printed words (e.g., [ I LIKE ], [ MUSIC ]).

How to play

  1. She taps [ I LIKE ] and then [ MUSIC ].
  2. The app speaks the full phrase aloud. This connects visual word building directly with artificial voice generation.

Level 3 → Level 4

From Whole Blocks to Tracing, Typing & Letter Production

Goal

Transitioning from selecting whole pre-made word blocks to building words out of letters via typing, tracing, or adaptive writing.

Rule

Never force traditional handwriting grips if motor fatigue occurs. Typing on a keyboard is 100% valid expressive writing.

1

Keyboard Target Typing (Visual Model Copying)

Setup

A physical computer keyboard or tablet with large-key layout. Place a clear word card (J-U-I-C-E) on top of the screen/monitor.

How to play

  1. She looks at the target card model and types the corresponding letters one by one into a large-font text editor.
  2. Every time she completes the word, hit the spacebar and have the screen read it aloud!
2

Adaptive Grip Tracing (Whiteboard / Sand Trays)

Setup

Use thick, weighted markers, egg-shaped crayons, or a finger in a tray of colored sand/shaving cream.

How to play

  1. Write her name or top target word (PARK) in large, thick yellow lines.
  2. Have her trace directly over the yellow lines using a dark marker or her finger, following the directional lines.
3

Letter-Tile Word Assembly (Spelling Target Gestalts)

Setup

Magnetic alphabet letters on a fridge/board and a target word template card with letter cutouts/boxes.

How to play

  1. Give her the target word template [ B ] [ L ] [ U ] [ E ] [ Y ].
  2. She finds the matching loose magnetic letter tiles and drops them into each corresponding box to spell the word from left to right.

Level 4 → Level 5 · Phase 2 Mastery

From Copying to Independent Expressive Writing

Goal

Spontaneous creation. Removing visual models so she generates her own original written thoughts, answers questions, or crafts stories independently.

Rule

Support her with digital word prediction tools or keyboard setups so fine motor friction never stalls her communication.

1

Picture Prompt Writing with Word Prediction

Setup

Show a photo of a recent family trip, park visit, or her favorite character doing something funny. Provide a tablet keyboard equipped with word prediction (or a physical word-bank menu).

How to play

  1. Ask: "What happened here?"
  2. She uses the keyboard to type her thought (e.g., WE WENT PARK or BLUEY IS JUMPING) without looking at a pre-made model card.
2

"Ask Me Anything" Daily Digital Journaling

Setup

Set up a simple digital journal template with one daily question prompt, e.g. "Today I want to play..." or "I felt happy when..."

How to play

  1. She completes the sentence by typing or selecting words from a word bank, gradually building a multi-page journal of her own thoughts over time.
3

Interactive Story Co-Creation

Setup

Open a digital slide deck or blank booklet.

How to play

  1. Let her choose a picture for the page.
  2. Then, ask her to write what the character says.
  3. She types out a full multi-word Gestalt script (e.g., READY SET GO!) directly into a speech bubble on the slide.
